Horry Electric Demo

On Wednesday, November 4 at 10:00 AM ET we show Horry Electric a contact center that stays calm during a storm. A supervisor turns on Storm Mode with one button. An AI agent named Savannah then answers outage calls, finds the member's account, and signs them up for a text when power comes back. Bills and payments use their NextGen CIS billing system, and no one ever reads a card number out loud.

3. Taking payments with no card numbers

Anything a caller says or keys in on the phone reaches the AI as text. So Savannah never asks for a card number. She uses one of three ways to pay, and none of them lets a card number reach the AI, an agent, a recording or our service.

MethodHow it worksStatus
Card on fileSavannah reads back the card ending and the amount. On a yes, Meridian's payment gateway charges the saved card.built
Secure text linkThe member gets a link and pays on their phone. Only a token comes back to us, and Savannah confirms the payment went through.built
Phone keypadTwilio Pay collects the digits outside the AI, then hands the caller back. This needs its own PCI-mode Twilio account.to do
Agent hands off to payA live agent sends the caller to secure pay, and the call comes back to the same agent when it's done.to do
The PCI message. Horry probably uses SAQ D today, since agents hear card numbers. With this design their contact center never handles a card number, so they add no PCI scope. Their auditor decides the final SAQ.

4. Web chat and email

Web chat: AI first, then a live agent

This works like the Infinity demo. A chat bubble sits on a copy of horryelectric.com. Savannah answers in text with the same tools she uses on the phone: account lookup, outage status, billing and the payment link. When the member asks for a person, or Savannah can't help, the chat moves to a live agent with the full history. Savannah's part never has to be repeated.

Email: a pick board instead of a ticket queue

We think "email ticketing" means they want emails to be tracked and owned, not left in a shared inbox. Our pick system already does that for calls in the Crete demo, and we can reuse it for email.

  • Emails to the co-op become cards on a board with the member's account, the topic (outage, billing, start or stop service) and how long the email has waited.
  • An agent picks an email and it opens on their desktop with the member's account details. A supervisor can assign an email to a specific agent.
  • AI drafts a reply from the member's account. For example, "your payment of $142.18 posted on 10/28". The agent edits and sends it.
  • On storm days the board groups emails by outage, so one reply about the outage can answer many members.
[CONFIRM] discovery call. Ask what "email ticketing" means to them before we build the full board. Today their email may be a shared inbox, or it may come from a web form.
