On Wednesday, November 4 at 10:00 AM ET we show Horry Electric a contact center that stays calm during a storm. A supervisor turns on Storm Mode with one button. An AI agent named Savannah then answers outage calls, finds the member's account, and signs them up for a text when power comes back. Bills and payments use their NextGen CIS billing system, and no one ever reads a card number out loud.

5. Taking payments with no card numbers

Anything a caller says or keys in on the phone reaches the AI as text. So Savannah never asks for a card number. She uses one of three ways to pay, and none of them lets a card number reach the AI, an agent, a recording or our service.

MethodHow it worksStatus
Card on fileSavannah reads back the card ending and the amount. On a yes, Meridian's payment gateway charges the saved card.built
Secure text linkThe member gets a link and pays on their phone. Only a token comes back to us, and Savannah confirms the payment went through.built
Phone keypad (demo)Savannah pauses. The WFC AI Controller collects the keypad digits and sends them straight to Meridian's payment gateway, then Savannah comes back with the result. The digits never reach the AI.to do
Phone keypad (production)A PCI-certified payment service collects the digits instead, so neither WFC nor Horry handles card data.talk
Agent hands off to payA live agent sends the caller to secure pay, and the call comes back to the same agent when it's done.to do
The PCI message. Horry told us they are SAQ D today and want to stay that way. With this design their new contact center never handles a card number, so it adds nothing to their PCI scope. Their auditor confirms this.

How a PCI-certified payment service works (the production option)

In production, the keypad step moves to a PCI-certified payment service (PCI DSS Level 1, the highest level). The card number goes from the caller's keypad to that service and on to the payment processor. Horry, WFC, Savannah and the agent only ever get the result.

  1. The member chooses to pay by keypad. Savannah, or the live agent, hands the call to the payment service.
  2. The service plays the prompts and collects the card number, expiry date, security code and ZIP code from the keypad. If a live agent stays on the call, they hear flat tones instead of the digits.
  3. The service sends the card straight to the payment processor, which charges the card or returns a token that stands in for it.
  4. Only the result comes back: approved or declined, the confirmation number and the last four digits. The call returns to Savannah or the same agent, who confirms the payment and notes the account.
What it needsNotes
A service that works with Meridian's gatewayWhich processor or token types Meridian takes decides which service fits. Horry may also already have a payment line through their billing vendor. Both are questions for the discovery call.
Keypad tones carried cleanly end to endThe tones have to pass through as signals (RFC 2833), not as sound, all the way to the payment service.
Recording paused during payment[CONFIRM] The call still passes through Webex Contact Center, so the WxCC recording must pause while the member keys in the card. This applies to the demo path too.

6. Web chat and email

Web chat: AI first, then a live agent

This works like the Infinity demo. A chat bubble sits on a copy of horryelectric.com. Savannah answers in text with the same tools she uses on the phone: account lookup, outage status, billing and the payment link. When the member asks for a person, or Savannah can't help, the chat moves to a live agent with the full history. Savannah's part never has to be repeated.

Decided. An escalated chat lands in our desktop widget, the same way as the Infinity demo.

Email: a pick board instead of a ticket queue

We think "email ticketing" means they want emails to be tracked and owned, not left in a shared inbox. Our pick system already does that for calls in the Crete demo, and we can reuse it for email.

  • Emails to the co-op become cards on a board with the member's account, the topic (outage, billing, start or stop service) and how long the email has waited.
  • An agent picks an email and it opens on their desktop with the member's account details. A supervisor can assign an email to a specific agent.
  • AI drafts a reply from the member's account. For example, "your payment of $142.18 posted on 10/28". The agent edits and sends it.
  • On storm days the board groups emails by outage, so one reply about the outage can answer many members.
[CONFIRM] discovery call. Ask what "email ticketing" means to them before we build the full board. Today their email may be a shared inbox, or it may come from a web form.

Test A: a normal day (Storm Mode off)

  1. Storm Console: set the presenter's phone to play David Nguyen (billing questions).
  2. Supervisor: open Storm Operations and check Storm Mode shows off.
  3. Billing agent: set the Desktop to Available.
  4. Call (972) 430-9658 from the presenter's mobile.
  5. Expect: no AI. The call rings the billing agent from Q_Horry_Billing, and the call details show David's name and account number. Answer, talk, end the call and pick a wrap-up code.
If the AI answers in Test A, Storm Mode is still on. Turn it off in Storm Operations and call again.

Test B: storm day, the AI finishes the call

  1. Storm Console: set the phone to play Linda Hardee (in the Socastee outage).
  2. Supervisor: Storm Operations → turn Storm Mode on and confirm. Optionally save an announcement, for example "Crews from neighbouring co-ops are helping us tonight."
  3. Call the demo line. Expect: Savannah answers, greets Linda by name, says her area's outage is already reported, and gives the estimate (about 2½ hours).
  4. Say yes when she offers a text when power is back, then let her end the call.
  5. Expect: no agent's phone rings. The Storm Console shows the conversation (section 06), and Linda in restoration notifications (section 04). Call Review shows the call as resolved by the AI.
  6. Storm Console section 03: Mark restored on the Socastee outage. Expect: the "power is back" text appears in section 05 (and on the phone once texting is set up).

Test C: storm day, wire down goes to a person

  1. Leave Storm Mode on. Storm Console: set the phone to play Marcus Jordan (on life support, same outage).
  2. Supervisor: Storm Operations → outage queue staffing → tick KC_Horry_Billing → Apply. This is the moment that answers Horry's concern 3: other departments join the outage queue with one click.
  3. Billing agent: Available.
  4. Call and say "There's a power line down across my driveway." Expect: Savannah tells him to stay at least 35 feet away, says one line about connecting him, and hands the call back.
  5. Expect: the billing agent's Desktop rings from Q_Horry_Outage at top priority. The call details show urgency emergency, the reason, and Savannah's summary, so Marcus doesn't repeat himself.
  6. Supervisor: open Call Review and click the call to show both parts: what Savannah did and the agent's part.

After each run

  • Supervisor: outage queue staffing → Return to normal (takes KC_Horry_Billing back off the outage queue).
  • Supervisor: turn Storm Mode off. Storm Operations warns if extra teams are still on the outage queue.
  • Storm Console: Reset demo data (re-creates the outages and clears calls and texts).
